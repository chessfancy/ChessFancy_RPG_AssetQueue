# Chapter 2 Region Art Bible — Thung Lũng Trung Cuộc

Status: world-building direction approved; screenplay still receiving line-by-line polish.

## Regional visual thesis

Chapter 2 should feel like one continuous highland frontier whose habitable area has contracted downward over years.

The visual progression is:

`crowded temporary settlement -> last inhabited fringe -> exposed wind station -> abandoned belt -> final patrol line -> invading forest -> root-dominated threshold -> swallowed ancient highland settlement`

The chapter must communicate the retreat of people through space before dialogue explains the cause.

## Shared visual language

- Vietnamese-fantasy mountain frontier
- top-down / high-oblique JRPG exploration view
- warm grass, weathered stone, earth, aged timber, cloth awnings and practical ropework
- distant blue mountain layers and mist for depth
- clear walkable paths and readable collision edges
- handmade infrastructure, not modern industrial machinery
- temporary settlement materials become rarer as elevation increases
- Xương Cuồng influence begins subtly with roots/vegetation, then dominates later maps

## Population gradient

Map 1 is intentionally crowded in open areas but must keep chokepoints sparse.
Map 2 still feels inhabited and supplied.
Map 3 is functional infrastructure with little residential clutter.
Map 4 shows layers of abandonment.
Map 5 reaches the final human patrol markers.
Map 6 retains only scattered human traces.
Map 7 has no human-made road or active settlement.
Map 8 reveals the large ancient settlement that was swallowed.

## Story-critical landmarks

- large red-map board / table at Trạm Giữ Tuyến
- wind-reading ancient Cối Xay / Trạm Gió
- abandoned Sơn Tinh shrine in the retreat belt
- patrol marker 17 at Sườn Núi Từng Bước
- visible giant Xương Cuồng root in the mountain reveal
- three root-anchor locations in the second half
- ancient ruined settlement + monumental Xương Cuồng tree at the final plateau

## Cối Xay

The Cối Xay is an ancient intelligent communal device, not a villain identity and not a metaphorical “plan grinder.” It grinds food and reads highland wind conditions for patrol/build teams. Its battle is a runaway-control event when it loses control.

## Sơn Tinh shrine

The abandoned shrine appears before the real Sơn Tinh encounter. It should feel formerly cared for, then gradually neglected as the inhabited frontier moved downward. It must support the later emotional correction: the population suspects abandonment, but Sơn Tinh says, “Ta chưa từng ruồng bỏ họ.”

## Xương Cuồng ecology

Do not start with horror. Progression:
- isolated roots in old fields
- roots breaking paths and stone
- forest crowding former buildings
- thick root networks and torn earth
- monumental ancient wood/root mass in the final zone

Child-friendly fantasy: ominous and powerful, not gore, rot-body horror or corpse imagery.

## Post-boss state

Do not instantly restore the plateau to lush perfection. Show restrained recovery:
- normal wind returns
- exposed hostile roots contract
- a thin stream resumes in an old stone channel
- grass/leaves begin moving normally
- distant birds return
- an old stone route behind the highland becomes visible
